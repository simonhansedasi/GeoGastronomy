# Funding Opportunities: Coffee Suitability DTS + ML + CMIP6 Project (Big Island, Hawaiʻi)

**Project summary:** 3-year research combining distributed temperature sensing (DTS/fiber-optic) field deployment, machine learning, and CMIP6 climate projections to map current and future coffee suitability in Kona and Kaʻu districts. Integrates terrain, ERA5-Land, Sentinel-2 NDVI, and SSURGO soil data. Core finding: Kona thermal niche contracts under +1°C; Kaʻu expands. Includes grower extension outputs and open data archiving. Lead institution: University of Hawaiʻi CTAHR.

**Research conducted:** April 2026. All deadlines and award amounts must be verified against current NOFOs before submission.

## Category 1: USDA NIFA Programs

### 1a. Specialty Crop Research Initiative (SCRI)
- **Administering agency:** USDA National Institute of Food and Agriculture (NIFA)
- **Typical award size (3-year project):** Up to $1,000,000/year per project (standard projects); up to $2,000,000/year for Coordinated Agricultural Projects (CAPs). Requests below $250,000 total are discouraged. Program-wide pool: ~$75–80 million/year.
- **Project types:** Standard Research and Extension Projects (SREPs), Coordinated Agricultural Projects (CAPs), Research and Extension Planning Projects (~$50,000, not for 3-year research)
- **Eligibility:** Federal agencies, national laboratories, colleges and universities, research institutions, private organizations, State Agricultural Experiment Stations, and individuals. UH CTAHR is fully eligible as lead.
- **Fit rating:** 5/5 — This is the highest-fit program. Coffee is a specialty crop by definition. The program explicitly requires transdisciplinary integrated research and extension, matching the grower extension component. Climate adaptation and production system research are funded priorities. The two-stage application (pre-application with Stakeholder Relevance Statement, then invited full application) rewards projects with demonstrated grower and industry engagement.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** FY2025 pre-applications closed November 8, 2024. FY2026 cycle pre-application deadline expected fall 2025 (likely October–November). Check nifa.usda.gov for the FY2026 NOFO.
- **Notes:** The SCRI requires a transdisciplinary team and extension deliverables — both are strengths of this project. A Coordinated Agricultural Project (CAP) structure could also accommodate multi-institution collaboration if CTAHR partners with another university. Emphasize economic impacts on growers, especially in the context of climate niche shift projections.

### 1b. Agriculture and Food Research Initiative (AFRI) — Foundational and Applied Science (FAS)
- **Administering agency:** USDA NIFA
- **Typical award size (3-year project):** Most awards $500,000–$750,000 total for standard grants; larger integrated grants possible. FY2026 total program pool: ~$140 million. Award duration: 12–60 months depending on grant type.
- **Eligibility:** Colleges, universities, research foundations, federal agencies, national labs, and other eligible entities. Individual PIs at UH are eligible.
- **Fit rating:** 3/5 — Best fit under the "Bioenergy, Natural Resources, and Environment" priority area (climate and agroecosystem work) or "Agriculture Systems and Technology" (remote sensing, ML). However, AFRI FAS tends to reward fundamental mechanistic science over applied place-based studies. The DTS instrumentation and ML components strengthen fit; the specialty crop/extension angle is less central to AFRI FAS than to SCRI.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** FY2026 deadlines: most priority areas December 2025; Postdoctoral Fellowships April 30, 2026; Predoctoral Fellowships April 16, 2026. Check nifa.usda.gov/grants/programs/agriculture-food-research-initiative/afri-deadlines.
- **Notes:** If submitting to AFRI, frame as a fundamental advance in agroecosystem response to warming using novel DTS measurement and CMIP6 downscaling. The ML-NDVI-DTS integration would be the scientific novelty hook. A 1:1 cost share may be required but may be waivable for universities.

### 1c. Organic Agriculture Research and Extension Initiative (OREI)
- **Administering agency:** USDA NIFA
- **Typical award size (3-year project):** Tier 1 Integrated: up to $3,500,000; Tier 2: up to $2,000,000; Tier 3: up to $1,000,000. FY2026 pool: ~$66 million.
- **Eligibility:** Same as SCRI — universities, nonprofits, research institutions.
- **Fit rating:** 2/5 — Coffee in Hawaii is not primarily organic-certified at scale, and the project does not center organic production systems. Unless the project specifically frames climate projections in terms of organic coffee expansion (e.g., Kaʻu organic transition potential), this is a poor fit. Planning and Workshop NOFOs ($50,000–$75,000) could fund a preliminary phase.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** Varies by NOFO; FY2026 NOFOs active. Check NIFA website.
- **Notes:** If a strong organic-transition angle can be built into the Kaʻu expansion findings (Kaʻu has growing organic coffee production), fit improves to 3/5.

### 1d. NRCS Conservation Innovation Grants (CIG)
- **Administering agency:** USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS)
- **Typical award size:** National CIG: project-dependent, typically $100,000–$2,000,000; On-Farm Trials component: up to $50 million/year program-wide, individual awards variable. State-level CIG awards often $5,000–$50,000.
- **Eligibility:** State and local governments, nonprofits, educational institutions, and others. Universities eligible.
- **Fit rating:** 2/5 — CIG focuses on farmer-direct conservation practice adoption and on-farm trials, not remote-sensing or modeling research per se. The DTS field deployment component has some relevance, but this project's primary output is knowledge/models, not a new conservation practice deployed on farms.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** National CIG follows EQIP batching deadline (January 15, 2026 recently announced); state CIG cycles vary. Check nrcs.usda.gov.
- **Notes:** A better angle would be a subsequent Phase 2 project once the suitability maps are complete — CIG could fund grower-facing tools derived from this research, not the research itself.

## Category 2: NOAA Programs

### 2a. NOAA Climate Program Office (CPO) — Competitive Research Grants
- **Administering agency:** NOAA Climate Program Office
- **Typical award size:** $50,000–$300,000/year per award for most competitions. Approximately $11 million available across ~90 awards in recent FY2023 competition.
- **Program areas relevant to this project:** Modeling, Analysis, Predictions and Projections (MAPP); Climate and Societal Interactions (CSI) — particularly the Food, Water, and Society theme.
- **Eligibility:** Universities, nonprofits, federal agencies, state agencies.
- **Fit rating:** 3/5 — The CMIP6 downscaling and climate projection components fit NOAA's core mission well. The Food, Water, and Society theme (under CSI) directly addresses agriculture-climate coupling. The DTS field component and ML modeling are differentiating strengths. Award size is modest relative to project scope; consider applying for the climate projection/modeling component alone.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** CPO typically releases one omnibus NOFO per fiscal year (often January–April). Check cpo.noaa.gov/Funding-Opportunities for the FY2026 announcement.
- **Notes:** CPO applications are highly competitive (sub-20% award rate). The Pacific Islands angle and the documented thermal niche shift finding could be positioned as high-priority under NOAA's climate impacts portfolio, especially if framed around food security and agricultural adaptation.

### 2b. NOAA Climate Adaptation Partnerships (CAP, formerly RISA) — Pacific Islands (Pacific RISA)
- **Administering agency:** NOAA Climate Program Office / Pacific RISA (hosted at UH SOEST)
- **Typical award size:** Core team awards approximately $1–2 million over 5 years; supplemental project funding $50,000–$200,000.
- **Eligibility:** Research institutions, universities, government agencies across U.S. Pacific Island jurisdictions.
- **Fit rating:** 3/5 — Pacific RISA explicitly covers Hawaiʻi and has agriculture as a key sector focus area. However, PI-CASC (the USGS-hosted partner center) has lost federal funding as of October 1, 2025, and Pacific RISA has faced uncertainty under current federal climate program cuts. The agricultural adaptation and grower extension elements are strong fits. Recommend checking whether Pacific RISA is issuing supplemental project calls.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** Pacific RISA supplemental project calls are irregular; contact the Pacific RISA team at UH SOEST directly (pacificrisa.org).
- **Notes:** Pacific RISA funding is not open-competition in the traditional sense — new core team awards require a full NOFO competition cycle (multi-year). Supplemental/collaborative project calls are the accessible entry point for this project.

### 2c. NOAA Sea Grant — Hawaii Sea Grant
- **Administering agency:** NOAA Sea Grant / Hawaii Sea Grant (UH SOEST)
- **Typical award size:** $100,000–$400,000 per project for standard research grants; Hawaii Sea Grant recently received $4.25 million for a resilience-focused cycle.
- **Eligibility:** University researchers (particularly at UH), nonprofits, state agencies.
- **Fit rating:** 2/5 — Sea Grant focuses on coastal and marine systems, fisheries, and ocean-related topics. Coffee agriculture in the interior highlands (Kona and Kaʻu are above the coast) is not a natural fit. Climate resilience framing could improve relevance marginally, but this project would compete against truly marine/coastal proposals.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** Hawaii Sea Grant runs competitive cycles every 2 years; check seagrant.soest.hawaii.edu for the next RFP.
- **Notes:** If the coastal coffee farms of Kona are specifically framed as part of a coastal agricultural system (sea-breeze microclimate coupling, coastal fog-drip contribution to thermal niche), fit improves slightly. But this is a stretch.

## Category 3: NSF Programs

### 3a. NSF Geography and Spatial Sciences (GSS)
- **Administering agency:** NSF Directorate for Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences (SBE)
- **Typical award size:** $40,000–$400,000 total for standard research awards (entire project duration). Program pool: $5–6 million/year; expects 25–30 awards.
- **Eligibility:** University faculty, research staff, and institutions. Individual PI awards permitted.
- **Fit rating:** 4/5 — Excellent thematic fit. GSS funds geospatial analysis, climate-landscape interactions, and human-environment systems. The DTS spatial network, Sentinel-2 NDVI integration, terrain analysis, and CMIP6-driven suitability mapping are classic GSS-eligible work. Award size is modest; this program is best suited to fund a component of the larger project (e.g., the spatial modeling and climate projection phase) or the full project if scoped to one PI.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** GSS accepts proposals on a rolling/annual basis with a typical deadline in late fall (historically November). Check nsf.gov for current solicitation (look under SBE).
- **Notes:** Consider a co-submission with an AFRI or SCRI award to cover the grower extension and field instrumentation costs that GSS would not fund at this budget level. A CAREER award ($400,000–$550,000) is also possible if the PI is pre-tenure.

### 3c. NSF Convergence Accelerator
- **Administering agency:** NSF Technology, Innovation and Partnerships (TIP)
- **Typical award size:** Phase 1: up to $750,000 for 12 months; Phase 2: up to $5,000,000 over approximately 3 years (invited only after Phase 1).
- **Eligibility:** Universities, nonprofits, private sector, government entities.
- **Fit rating:** 2/5 — The Convergence Accelerator funds topic-specific tracks announced annually; applicants must respond to a designated track topic. As of early 2026, no active track directly addresses agricultural climate adaptation or specialty crops. The program is also being expanded regionally. Fit is opportunistic — if a future track (e.g., food systems resilience, AI for climate adaptation) is announced, this project could be competitive in Phase 1.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** Track topics announced each spring; Phase 1 proposals typically due summer–fall. Monitor nsf.gov/funding/initiatives/convergence-accelerator.
- **Notes:** The ML and open-data-archiving components of this project fit the Convergence Accelerator's emphasis on use-inspired, translational science. Worth monitoring for a matching track announcement.

- **Administering agency:** NSF
- **Typical award size:** Research Infrastructure Improvement (RII) Track-1: $4–6 million over 5 years (state-level); Focused EPSCoR Collaborations (FEC): smaller, interjurisdictional. Individual project awards under EPSCoR depend on the state program.

- **Fit rating:** 3/5 — Hawaii EPSCoR is actively building a climate and AI/ML research portfolio (the "Change Hawaiʻi" project). This project's combination of ML, climate projections, and field sensing aligns well with the state's S&T plan being developed for 2026. Fit is contingent on whether UH EPSCoR has a current competitive call that matches this topic area.
- **Next deadline / typical cycle:** Hawaii EPSCoR manages state-level competitions on a rolling basis. Contact UH EPSCoR office (hawaii.edu/epscor) for current internal solicitations. NSF-level FEC proposals have their own solicitation cycle.

## Recommended Strategy

**Priority 1 — SCRI (USDA NIFA):** This is the single strongest fit. Coffee is a specialty crop; the project integrates research and extension; CTAHR is an ideal lead institution. Aim for a Coordinated Agricultural Project if additional university partners (e.g., mainland climatology or remote sensing group) can be engaged, which increases the award ceiling substantially. The pre-application Stakeholder Relevance Statement should feature grower associations from both Kona and Kaʻu.

**Priority 2 — AFRI FAS (USDA NIFA) or GSS (NSF):** Submit to one of these concurrently with or shortly after SCRI. AFRI FAS funds the fundamental science angle (DTS microclimate measurement, ML downscaling, CMIP6 ensemble analysis); GSS funds the spatial/geographic analysis and human-environment dimension. They are not mutually exclusive with SCRI.

**Priority 3 — Build preliminary data first (UH/CTAHR internal):** A 12-month internal seed grant to deploy the DTS network and generate the first season of microclimate data makes the project competitive in every federal program above. Federal reviewers expect preliminary results.

**Priority 4 — Industry partnership (WCR):** Engage WCR as a named collaborator in the SCRI application. Their involvement strengthens industry relevance scoring and may result in cost-sharing support that improves overall project budget.

**Watch for:** NSF Convergence Accelerator track announcements (spring annually); NOAA CPO omnibus NOFO (spring 2026); any restoration of PI-CASC funding under USGS.

## Important Caveats

1. Several federal programs (PI-CASC, some CPO programs, DISES) have faced funding uncertainty or termination under current federal budget conditions (2025–2026). Verify program status before investing proposal effort.
2. All deadlines listed are based on prior-year cycles and web sources as of April 2026. Always confirm against the current NOFO on Grants.gov or the agency's program page.
3. SCRI pre-applications for FY2026 likely closed in fall 2025 if the cycle followed the FY2025 pattern (November 8, 2024 deadline). The FY2027 cycle would be the next SCRI entry point if FY2026 has passed.
4. AFRI FY2026 deadlines (most areas: December 2025) may also have passed. Confirm current status and look for FY2027 NOFOs.
